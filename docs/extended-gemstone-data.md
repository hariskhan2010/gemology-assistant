# Extended Gemstone Property Database

## For AI-Assisted Gemstone Identification

This reference document catalogs physical and optical properties for 80+ gemstone species and varieties, organized by mineral group. Data is drawn from GIA, Gemological Institute of America, and standard gemmological reference texts. Values listed are for standard faceted or cabochon gem materials unless noted. All RI values are taken with sodium light (589.3 nm) unless specified otherwise. This document is intended as an authoritative reference for AI-driven gemstone identification workflows, including RAG-based retrieval and automated property matching.

## 1.1 Corundum Group (Extended)

### Ruby

- **Refractive Index:** 1.762–1.770
- **Birefringence:** 0.008
- **Specific Gravity:** 3.99–4.01
- **Mohs Hardness:** 9
- **Crystal System:** Trigonal
- **Optic Character:** Uniaxial
- **Optic Sign:** Negative (−)
- **Pleochroism:** Purplish-red / orangey-red (strong)
- **UV Fluorescence LW:** Strong red (Burma), moderate red (Sri Lanka), inert (Thai)
- **UV Fluorescence SW:** Moderate red (Burma), weak to inert (Thai)
- **Chelsea Colour Filter Reaction:** Red (natural), bright red (synthetic)
- **Key Inclusions:** Silk (rutile needles), hexagonal growth zoning, fingerprint fluid inclusions, twinning planes, calcite crystals, mica flakes
- **Synthetic Detection:** Curved striae (Verneuil flame-fusion), flux particles and residual flux (flux-growth), chevron growth zoning (hydrothermal), gas bubbles
- **Common Treatments:** Heat (1100–1900°C to dissolve silk and improve color), lead-glass filling (fracture healing), beryllium surface diffusion, flux healing of fissures
- **Geographic Origins:** Myanmar (Mogok, Mong Hsu), Thailand (Chanthaburi), Mozambique (Montepuez), Sri Lanka (Ratnapura), Madagascar (Ilakaka), Tanzania (Winza), Vietnam (Luc Yen)
- **Care Notes:** Ultrasonic and steam cleaning safe for untreated stones; avoid for glass-filled or diffusion-treated stones.

### Sapphire — Blue

- **Refractive Index:** 1.762–1.770
- **Birefringence:** 0.008
- **Specific Gravity:** 3.99–4.01
- **Mohs Hardness:** 9
- **Crystal System:** Trigonal
- **Optic Character:** Uniaxial
- **Optic Sign:** Negative (−)
- **Pleochroism:** Blue / bluish-green (strong)
- **UV Fluorescence LW:** Usually inert; weak red/orange (Sri Lanka, Montana)
- **UV Fluorescence SW:** Usually inert
- **Chelsea Colour Filter Reaction:** Greenish or dark (most), red (some Sri Lankan)
- **Key Inclusions:** Silk (fine rutile needles), color zoning (angular/hexagonal), fingerprint inclusions, boehmite needles, mineral crystals (zircon, apatite, feldspar)
- **Synthetic Detection:** Curved striae (Verneuil), gas bubbles, color banding curved rather than straight, Pt or Ir flux particles (flux-growth)
- **Common Treatments:** Heat (to remove silk, lighten or deepen color), lattice diffusion (Ti, Cr, Be surface diffusion), lead-glass fracture filling, beryllium bulk diffusion
- **Geographic Origins:** Kashmir (India, now depleted), Myanmar (Mogok), Sri Lanka, Madagascar (Ilakaka, Andranondambo), Thailand (Kanchanaburi), Australia (Queensland), Montana (Yogo Gulch), Tanzania (Umba River), Nigeria (Jos)
- **Care Notes:** Heat-treated stones are generally stable; diffusion-treated stones may chip or show uneven color if repolished.

### Sapphire — Pink / Padparadscha

- **Refractive Index:** 1.762–1.770
- **Birefringence:** 0.008
- **Specific Gravity:** 3.99–4.01
- **Mohs Hardness:** 9
- **Crystal System:** Trigonal
- **Optic Character:** Uniaxial
- **Optic Sign:** Negative (−)
- **Pleochroism:** Pink / orange-pink (Padparadscha: orange / pink), moderate to strong
- **UV Fluorescence LW:** Moderate red (pink), orange-red (Padparadscha)
- **UV Fluorescence SW:** Weak to moderate
- **Chelsea Colour Filter Reaction:** Weak red to pink
- **Key Inclusions:** Silk, zircon crystals, color zoning, polished tube cavities
- **Synthetic Detection:** Curved striae, gas bubbles, color zoning anomalies
- **Common Treatments:** Heat (pink to deepen color), beryllium diffusion (to create padparadscha color)
- **Geographic Origins:** Sri Lanka (Ratnapura, Elahera), Madagascar, Myanmar, Tanzania (Umba Valley), Vietnam (Luc Yen)
- **Care Notes:** Standard corundum care; padparadscha color is rare naturally, so verify treatment origin.

### Sapphire — Yellow, Green, Purple, Fancy

- **Refractive Index:** 1.762–1.770
- **Birefringence:** 0.008
- **Specific Gravity:** 3.99–4.01
- **Mohs Hardness:** 9
- **Crystal System:** Trigonal
- **Optic Character:** Uniaxial
- **Optic Sign:** Negative (−)
- **Pleochroism:** Yellow: yellow / yellow-orange (weak); green: green / yellow-green (moderate); purple: violet-purple / orange-purple (strong)
- **UV Fluorescence LW:** Variable: yellow often inert, green often inert, purple may show red
- **Chelsea Colour Filter Reaction:** Variable by color
- **Key Inclusions:** Color zoning, rutile silk, mineral inclusions, growth structures
- **Synthetic Detection:** Same methods as blue/pink corundum
- **Common Treatments:** Heat, lattice diffusion (yellow and orange)
- **Geographic Origins:** Sri Lanka (yellow, green, purple), Australia (green), Thailand (yellow), Madagascar (multi-color), Tanzania (multi-color)
- **Care Notes:** Color-change corundum exists (blue/violet to purple/red); treat per standard corundum care.

### Synthetic Corundum

- **Refractive Index:** 1.762–1.770 (may vary slightly with dopants)
- **Birefringence:** 0.008
- **Specific Gravity:** 3.99–4.01
- **Mohs Hardness:** 9
- **Crystal System:** Trigonal
- **Optic Character:** Uniaxial
- **Optic Sign:** Negative (−)
- **Pleochroism:** Same as natural colors but often weaker
- **UV Fluorescence LW:** Strong red (synthetic ruby, Cr-doped), variable per color
- **UV Fluorescence SW:** Often stronger than natural equivalents
- **Key Diagnostic Features:** Curved striae (flame fusion / Verneuil), gas bubbles, dense flux particles (flux method), metallic inclusions from crucibles (Czochralski), no natural silk or mineral crystals
- **Growth Methods:** Verneuil (flame fusion) — most common; Czochralski (pulled) — high optical quality; Flux-growth (Chatham, Ramaura) — natural-looking inclusions; Hydrothermal (Tairus) — Russian-grown, good color
- **Common Dopants:** Cr (red/pink), Fe + Ti (blue), Ni (yellow), Co (blue-green), V (color-change)
- **Care Notes:** Identical physical properties to natural corundum; clean with normal methods.

## 1.2 Beryl Group (Extended)

### Emerald

- **Refractive Index:** 1.565–1.598
- **Birefringence:** 0.005–0.009
- **Specific Gravity:** 2.67–2.78
- **Mohs Hardness:** 7.5–8
- **Crystal System:** Hexagonal
- **Optic Character:** Uniaxial
- **Optic Sign:** Negative (−)
- **Pleochroism:** Bluish-green / yellowish-green (moderate to strong)
- **UV Fluorescence LW:** Usually inert; weak green (low-iron, Colombian)
- **UV Fluorescence SW:** Usually inert; weak green (some Colombian)
- **Chelsea Colour Filter Reaction:** Red / pink (Colombian), pinkish (Zambian), green (low-Cr Brazilian)
- **Key Inclusions:** Three-phase inclusions (liquid + gas + crystal, hallmark of Colombian), two-phase inclusions, mica flakes, pyrite crystals, calcite, actinolite needles (Zambian), growth tubes, oil-filled fissures, fingerprint patterns
- **Synthetic Detection:** Flux and hydrothermal synthetics; look for flux particles (platinum, gold, copper), wispy veil-like flux residues, chevron growth zoning (hydrothermal), natural-appearing but too-clean inclusions
- **Common Treatments:** Fracture filling with cedarwood oil, Opticon, or epoxy resin; minor heat; surface coating (rare); dyeing (rare)
- **Geographic Origins:** Colombia (Muzo, Chivor, Coscuez), Zambia (Kafubu, Ndola), Brazil (Itabira, Capoeirana), Ethiopia (Shakiso), Afghanistan (Panshir), Pakistan (Swat Valley), Madagascar, Zimbabwe (Sandawana), Russia (Urals), South Africa (Somerset)
- **Care Notes:** Very brittle — avoid ultrasonic and steam cleaning (may remove oil fillings). Clean with soft cloth, warm soapy water only. Impact-sensitive due to fissure networks.

- **Geographic Origins:** Brazil (Santa Maria, Marambaya, Minas Gerais), Nigeria, Madagascar, Mozambique, Zambia, Pakistan (Kashmir), Russia, China
- **Care Notes:** Durable; ultrasonic and steam safe for untreated stones. Heat-treated colors are stable.

## 1.3 Quartz Group (Extended)

### Amethyst

- **Refractive Index:** 1.544–1.553
- **Birefringence:** 0.009
- **Specific Gravity:** 2.64–2.69
- **Mohs Hardness:** 7
- **Crystal System:** Trigonal
- **Optic Character:** Uniaxial
- **Optic Sign:** Positive (+)
- **Pleochroism:** Violet / red-violet (weak to moderate)
- **UV Fluorescence LW:** Inert (usually)
- **UV Fluorescence SW:** Inert
- **Key Inclusions:** "Tiger stripes" (Brazil twinning), color zoning, goethite needles, hematite inclusions, three-phase inclusions, fingerprint patterns
- **Synthetic Detection:** Hydrothermal synthetic amethyst exists; look for precisely parallel growth lines, very uniform color, lack of twinning; may show metallic Fe inclusions
- **Common Treatments:** Heat (to produce citrine or green quartz), irradiation (to deepen color)
- **Geographic Origins:** Brazil (Rio Grande do Sul), Zambia (Miku), Uruguay (Artigas), Madagascar, Sri Lanka, Russia, Bolivia (Anahi, ametrine)
- **Care Notes:** Color may fade with prolonged UV exposure. Avoid high heat (changes color). Ultrasonic safe.

### Citrine

- **Refractive Index:** 1.544–1.553
- **Birefringence:** 0.009
- **Specific Gravity:** 2.64–2.69
- **Mohs Hardness:** 7
- **Crystal System:** Trigonal
- **Optic Character:** Uniaxial
- **Optic Sign:** Positive (+)
- **Pleochroism:** Yellow / brownish-yellow (weak)
- **UV Fluorescence LW:** Inert
- **UV Fluorescence SW:** Inert
- **Key Inclusions:** Usually very clean; may show hematite needles, growth tubes; most citrine is heat-treated amethyst (distinguish by dehydrated hematite inclusions in natural)
- **Synthetic Detection:** Rarely synthesized; hydrothermal citrine exists but scarce
- **Common Treatments:** Heat treatment of amethyst (400–500°C) is standard; irradiation to deepen color (rare)
- **Geographic Origins:** Brazil, Madagascar, Russia, Bolivia, Spain, France
- **Care Notes:** Durable; colors stable. Most commercial citrine is heat-treated amethyst.

### Ametrine

- **Refractive Index:** 1.544–1.553
- **Birefringence:** 0.009
- **Specific Gravity:** 2.64–2.69
- **Mohs Hardness:** 7
- **Crystal System:** Trigonal
- **Optic Character:** Uniaxial
- **Optic Sign:** Positive (+)
- **Pleochroism:** Violet + yellow zones (weak in each zone)
- **UV Fluorescence LW:** Inert
- **UV Fluorescence SW:** Inert
- **Key Inclusions:** Distinct color zoning with sharp boundaries, Brazil twinning, hematite inclusions
- **Synthetic Detection:** Hydrothermal ametrine exists; look for unnatural color boundary angles
- **Common Treatments:** Heat + irradiation from single amethyst crystal; natural ametrine only from Bolivia (Anahi mine)
- **Geographic Origins:** Bolivia (Anahi mine, only commercial source), Brazil (rare), India (trace)
- **Care Notes:** Durable; color boundary is structural and stable.

### Rock Crystal

- **Refractive Index:** 1.544–1.553
- **Birefringence:** 0.009
- **Specific Gravity:** 2.64–2.69
- **Mohs Hardness:** 7
- **Crystal System:** Trigonal
- **Optic Character:** Uniaxial
- **Optic Sign:** Positive (+)
- **Pleochroism:** None
- **UV Fluorescence LW:** Inert
- **UV Fluorescence SW:** Inert
- **Key Inclusions:** Fluid inclusions, growth lines, rutile needles (rutilated quartz), tourmaline crystals, chlorite, hematite, goethite, phantom growths, liquid inclusions

- **Geographic Origins:** Brazil (Minas Gerais), Madagascar, Switzerland (Alps), USA (Arkansas), Russia, India, China

### Smoky Quartz

- **Mohs Hardness:** 7
- **Crystal System:** Trigonal

- **Optic Sign:** Positive (+)

- **UV Fluorescence LW:** Inert
- **UV Fluorescence SW:** Inert

### Rose Quartz

- **Refractive Index:** 1.544–1.553
- **Birefringence:** 0.009
- **Specific Gravity:** 2.64–2.69
- **Mohs Hardness:** 7
- **Crystal System:** Trigonal (macrocrystalline); massive usual
- **Optic Character:** Uniaxial
- **Optic Sign:** Positive (+)
- **Pleochroism:** Weak to none
- **UV Fluorescence LW:** Weak pink or inert
- **UV Fluorescence SW:** Inert
- **Key Inclusions:** Rutile needles (asterism in cabochons), turbidity, fine inclusions causing milky or hazy appearance, three-phase inclusions
- **Synthetic Detection:** Hydrothermal synthetic rose quartz exists but rare
- **Common Treatments:** Heat to remove pink (uncommon); irradiation to deepen color (limited)
- **Geographic Origins:** Brazil (Minas Gerais, Bahia), Madagascar, India, South Africa, USA (South Dakota), Mozambique, Namibia
- **Care Notes:** Color may fade with prolonged light or heat exposure (some radiation-derived color). Often found in massive form.

### Chalcedony — Agate

- **Refractive Index:** 1.530–1.543 (spot reading typically ~1.54)
- **Birefringence:** Aggregate (not measurable in cutting)
- **Specific Gravity:** 2.58–2.64
- **Mohs Hardness:** 6.5–7
- **Crystal System:** Hexagonal (microcrystalline quartz)
- **Optic Character:** Aggregate
- **Optic Sign:** N/A
- **Pleochroism:** None
- **UV Fluorescence LW:** Variable; often inert
- **UV Fluorescence SW:** Variable; may be inert or weak
- **Key Diagnostic Features:** Banded structure, concentric or fortification patterns, moss-like or plume inclusions, dendritic patterns, color zoning
- **Synthetic Detection:** Dyed agate is common; check for unnatural color concentration in bands; synthetic agate (reconstructed) exists
- **Common Treatments:** Dyeing (with aniline or metallic salts), heat treatment (to produce red or brown), irradiation, sugar-acid treatment (for black agate / onyx), impregnation (with plastic or wax)
- **Geographic Origins:** Brazil (Rio Grande do Sul), Uruguay, India, Madagascar, Mexico, USA (Oregon, Washington, Montana), Botswana (Botswana agate)
- **Care Notes:** Durable; dyed colors may fade with UV exposure. Avoid strong heat. Ultrasonic safe.

## 1.4 Garnet Group (Extended)

### Pyrope

- **Refractive Index:** 1.730–1.760
- **Birefringence:** None (isotropic)
- **Specific Gravity:** 3.65–3.80
- **Mohs Hardness:** 7–7.5
- **Crystal System:** Cubic
- **Optic Character:** Isotropic
- **Optic Sign:** N/A
- **Pleochroism:** None
- **UV Fluorescence LW:** Inert (usually)
- **UV Fluorescence SW:** Inert
- **Chelsea Colour Filter Reaction:** Greenish (pyrope-almandine)
- **Key Inclusions:** Needles, rounded crystals (zircon, apatite), curved feathers, \"scalelike\" or \"treacle\" patterns (Cape ruby — pyrope from South Africa)
- **Synthetic Detection:** Pyrope is rarely synthesized; YAG and CZ are common simulants
- **Common Treatments:** None typical; heat to improve color (uncommon)
- **Geographic Origins:** South Africa (Kimberley), USA (Arizona — Ant Hill garnets), China, Tanzania, Sri Lanka, Brazil, Czech Republic (Bohemia)
- **Care Notes:** Durable; no cleavage, good for all jewelry. Often confused with ruby in antique pieces (\"Cape ruby\").

### Almandine

- **Common Treatments:** Heat (to enhance red color); fracture filling (uncommon)
- **Geographic Origins:** India (Rajasthan), Tanzania, Madagascar, Brazil, Sri Lanka, USA (Idaho, Alaska), Australia, Pakistan, Zambia

### Grossular (Tsavorite, Hessonite)

- **Refractive Index:** 1.730–1.760

- **Optic Character:** Isotropic
- **Optic Sign:** N/A

- **Key Inclusions:** Tsavorite: three-phase inclusions, apatite crystals, fingerprints; Hessonite: \"scalelike\" inclusion with oily or treacle-like appearance, wispy veils; Hydrogrossular: granular texture

- **Common Treatments:** Heat (Tsavorite: lighten to improve green)
- **Geographic Origins:** Tsavorite: Kenya (Tsavo), Tanzania (Merelani); Hessonite: Sri Lanka, Madagascar, Brazil, Canada, Pakistan; Hydrogrossular: Canada, South Africa
- **Care Notes:** Durable; Tsavorite valuable and hard; Hessonite may have brittle areas.

### Andradite — Demantoid

- **Refractive Index:** 1.855–1.895 (highest of garnets)
- **Birefringence:** None (isotropic)
- **Specific Gravity:** 3.80–3.88
- **Mohs Hardness:** 6.5–7
- **Crystal System:** Cubic
- **Optic Character:** Isotropic
- **Optic Sign:** N/A
- **Pleochroism:** None
- **UV Fluorescence LW:** Inert
- **UV Fluorescence SW:** Inert
- **Key Inclusions:** \"Horsetail\" inclusions — radiating asbestos (byssolite) fibers (diagnostic for Russian demantoid), gas bubbles, healed fractures

- **Geographic Origins:** Russia (Bobrovka, Urals), Namibia (Erongo), Italy (Val Malenco), Iran (rare)
- **Care Notes:** Relatively fragile due to hardness (6.5) and fracture-prone nature; use protective settings. The dispersion is higher than diamond (0.057).

## 1.10 Zircon (Extended)

### High Zircon

- **Refractive Index:** 1.925–1.985 (tetragonal)

- **Specific Gravity:** 4.60–4.70
- **Mohs Hardness:** 7–7.5
- **Crystal System:** Tetragonal
- **Optic Character:** Uniaxial
- **Optic Sign:** Positive (+)
- **Pleochroism:** Weak
- **UV Fluorescence LW:** Yellow to orange (some)

- **Key Diagnostic Features:** Very high RI, strong birefringence (double facets visible), high dispersion (0.039), \"paper-scratch\" effect (abraded facet edges), sharp double refraction visible under loupe
- **Synthetic Detection:** None (zircon is natural; CZ and synthetic rutile used as simulants)
- **Common Treatments:** Heat (to produce colorless, blue, golden, red from brownish natural); irradiation (to deepen color)

### Medium Zircon

- **Refractive Index:** 1.830–1.930 (varies with metamictization)
- **Birefringence:** 0.010–0.040
- **Specific Gravity:** 4.10–4.60
- **Mohs Hardness:** 6–7
- **Crystal System:** Tetragonal (partially metamict)
- **Key Diagnostic Features:** Lower RI, SG, and birefringence due to radiation damage; may show anomalous double refraction; cloudy or smoky appearance
- **Geographic Origins:** Sri Lanka, Myanmar, Cambodia

### Low Zircon

- **Mohs Hardness:** 5–6
- **Crystal System:** Amorphous (metamict)
- **Key Diagnostic Features:** Metamict — radiation damage has destroyed crystal structure; may be green or brown; dark, cloudy, with concentric color zoning, gives anomalous isotropic behavior
- **Common Treatments:** Heat can recrystallize structure to restore some properties
- **Geographic Origins:** Sri Lanka, Thailand, Cambodia, Myanmar, Nigeria
- **Care Notes:** Very fragile; avoid heat and physical stress.

### HPHT Synthetic Diamond

- **Refractive Index:** 2.417
- **Birefringence:** None (isotropic)
- **Specific Gravity:** 3.52
- **Mohs Hardness:** 10
- **Crystal System:** Cubic
- **Optic Character:** Isotropic
- **Pleochroism:** None
- **UV Fluorescence LW:** Blue, yellow, or green (strong, often patchy)
- **UV Fluorescence SW:** Strong, often zoned

### CVD Synthetic Diamond

- **Optic Character:** Isotropic
- **Pleochroism:** None
- **UV Fluorescence LW:** Strong orange-red or pink (common)
- **UV Fluorescence SW:** Strong orange-red, often patchy

- **Care Notes:** Standard diamond care; may show brownish color if unannealed.

### Jadeite

- **Refractive Index:** 1.654–1.673
- **Birefringence:** Aggregate (not measurable in cutting)

- **Mohs Hardness:** 6.5–7

- **Pleochroism:** None in aggregate

### Jet

- **Refractive Index:** 1.64–1.68 (spot; variable)
- **Birefringence:** None (amorphous)
- **Specific Gravity:** 1.30–1.35
- **Mohs Hardness:** 2.5–4
- **Crystal System:** Amorphous (fossilized wood)
- **Key Diagnostic Features:** Intense black, very light weight (SG ~1.33), warm to touch, brown streak, conchoidal fracture
- **Synthetic Detection:** Glass (cold, heavier), obsidian (heavier SG 2.4), vulcanite (smells of sulfur when hot)
- **Common Treatments:** Waxing, oiling
- **Geographic Origins:** England (Whitby), Spain, France, Germany, USA (Colorado, Utah), Turkey, India
- **Care Notes:** Very soft and brittle; avoid heat and chemicals. Clean with dry cloth.

### Moldavite

- **Refractive Index:** 1.488–1.527
- **Birefringence:** None (amorphous)
- **Specific Gravity:** 2.32–2.40
- **Mohs Hardness:** 5.5
- **Crystal System:** Amorphous (tektite)
- **Optic Character:** Isotropic
- **Pleochroism:** None
- **UV Fluorescence LW:** Inert
- **UV Fluorescence SW:** Inert
- **Key Diagnostic Features:** Green to brown-green color, sculpted surface (flow ridges, pitting), gas bubbles, dark glassy appearance
- **Synthetic Detection:** Glass imitations (too perfect surface, different SG)
- **Geographic Origins:** Czech Republic (Bohemia — Moldau River valley, the only source), Austria (rare)
- **Care Notes:** Durable but can chip; avoid rapid temperature change.

### Tektites

- **Refractive Index:** 1.48–1.52
- **Birefringence:** None (amorphous)
- **Specific Gravity:** 2.30–2.55
- **Mohs Hardness:** 5.5–6.5
- **Crystal System:** Amorphous
- **Optic Character:** Isotropic
- **Pleochroism:** None
- **Key Diagnostic Features:** Dark brown to black, splashform shapes (teardrops, buttons, dumbbells), schlieren flow lines
- **Geographic Origins:** Australia (australites), SE Asia (indochinites), Philippines, China, Ivory Coast, USA (Texas)
- **Care Notes:** Durable; clean normally.

### Hematite

- **Refractive Index:** 2.940–3.220 (opaque)
- **Birefringence:** 0.280 (opaque)
- **Specific Gravity:** 5.26
- **Mohs Hardness:** 5.5–6.5
- **Crystal System:** Trigonal
- **Pleochroism:** None (opaque)
- **Key Diagnostic Features:** Black with metallic luster, reddish-brown streak (diagnostic), heavy (SG 5.26), may show iridescent tarnish
- **Synthetic Detection:** "Magnetic hematite" is synthetic barium/strontium ferrite; natural is not magnetic
- **Common Treatments:** Heat, coating
- **Geographic Origins:** Brazil, Switzerland, England, USA (Lake Superior), Canada, Australia, Germany
- **Care Notes:** Can rust if exposed to water; wipe dry after cleaning.

### Pyrite

- **Refractive Index:** Opaque
- **Specific Gravity:** 4.95–5.10
- **Mohs Hardness:** 6–6.5
- **Crystal System:** Cubic
- **Pleochroism:** None (opaque)
- **Key Diagnostic Features:** Pale brassy-yellow, cubic crystals with striated faces, "fool's gold", greenish-black streak
- **Geographic Origins:** Peru, Spain, Italy, USA (Colorado, Illinois), Mexico, Bolivia, China
- **Care Notes:** Tarnishes and may rust; avoid moisture.

### Marcasite

- **Refractive Index:** Opaque
- **Specific Gravity:** 4.85–4.90
- **Mohs Hardness:** 6–6.5
- **Crystal System:** Orthorhombic (dimorph of pyrite)
- **Key Diagnostic Features:** Pale bronze-yellow, radiating/tabular habit, cock's comb twins, more unstable than pyrite
- **Geographic Origins:** Czech Republic, Austria, USA (Oklahoma, Missouri), Poland, Canada
- **Care Notes:** Unstable in moist air. Jewelry "marcasite" is typically pyrite set in silver.

### Obsidian

- **Refractive Index:** 1.48–1.51 (typical ~1.50)
- **Birefringence:** None (amorphous)
- **Specific Gravity:** 2.33–2.42 (typically 2.40)
- **Mohs Hardness:** 5–5.5
- **Crystal System:** Amorphous (volcanic glass)
- **Optic Character:** Isotropic
- **Pleochroism:** None
- **UV Fluorescence LW:** Variable; often inert
- **Key Diagnostic Features:** Volcanic glass, conchoidal fracture, sharp edges, typically black, may show banding or inclusions
- **Common Treatments:** Heat (to produce silver sheen), coating
- **Geographic Origins:** USA (Oregon, California, Wyoming), Mexico, Iceland, Italy, Russia, Armenia, Japan
- **Care Notes:** Moderate durability but can chip; conchoidal fracture makes edges sharp.

### Obsidian — Snowflake

- **Refractive Index:** 1.48–1.51
- **Birefringence:** None
- **Specific Gravity:** 2.33–2.42
- **Mohs Hardness:** 5–5.5
- **Crystal System:** Amorphous
- **Key Diagnostic Features:** Black glass with white radial clusters (spherulites) of cristobalite — "snowflakes"
- **Geographic Origins:** USA (Utah, Oregon), Mexico
- **Care Notes:** Same as obsidian.

### Obsidian — Rainbow / Mahogany / Sheen / Apache Tears

- **Refractive Index:** 1.48–1.51
- **Birefringence:** None
- **Specific Gravity:** 2.33–2.42
- **Mohs Hardness:** 5–5.5
- **Crystal System:** Amorphous
- **Key Diagnostic Features:** Rainbow: thin-film interference from magnetite; Mahogany: black with brown hematite swirls; Sheen: gold/silver reflective sheen; Apache Tears: small rounded black nodules
- **Geographic Origins:** USA (Arizona, New Mexico, Oregon, California), Mexico, Peru
- **Care Notes:** Same as obsidian.
