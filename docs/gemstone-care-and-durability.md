# Gemstone Care & Durability Guide

A comprehensive reference for gemstone durability, proper cleaning methods, species-specific care, storage, and treatment-specific handling.

---

## 1. Durability Fundamentals

Durability in gemology is assessed across three independent axes: hardness, toughness, and stability. A gem is only as durable as its weakest attribute.

### Hardness (Mohs Scale)

The Mohs hardness scale (1–10) measures a mineral's resistance to scratching, ranked by which minerals can scratch others. Invented by Friedrich Mohs in 1812, it is ordinal, not linear — diamond (10) is roughly 160× harder than corundum (9).

| Mohs | Mineral | Comparative |
|------|---------|-------------|
| 10 | Diamond | Hardest natural substance |
| 9 | Corundum (Ruby, Sapphire) | 160× softer than diamond |
| 8 | Topaz, Spinel, Beryl | |
| 7.5 | Zircon, Andalusite | |
| 7 | Quartz (Amethyst, Citrine), Tourmaline, Garnet | Typical "safe for daily wear" threshold |
| 6.5 | Peridot, Nephrite, Jadeite | |
| 6 | Moonstone, Feldspar, Opal | |
| 5 | Apatite, Lapis Lazuli, Turquoise | |
| 4 | Fluorite, Rhodochrosite | |
| 3.5 | Malachite, Coral | |
| 3 | Calcite, Pearl | |
| 2 | Amber, Gypsum | Fingernail can scratch |

### Toughness

Toughness measures a gem's resistance to breaking, chipping, or cracking under impact. It is independent of hardness:

- **Exceptional:** Nephrite, Jadeite, Diamond (despite perfect cleavage, its toughness is excellent), Sapphire, Ruby
- **Good:** Spinel, Quartz, Garnet, Topaz, Aquamarine
- **Fair:** Emerald, Peridot, Tourmaline, Tanzanite, Moonstone
- **Poor:** Opal, Turquoise, Malachite, Lapis Lazuli, Zircon (despite high hardness), Iolite

Emerald is a classic example: Mohs 7.5–8 (reasonably hard) but fair toughness due to natural fractures and inclusions. Zircon (Mohs 7.5) is brittle and chips easily at facet junctions.

### Stability

Stability describes a gem's resistance to heat, light, chemicals, and environmental exposure.

- **Heat-sensitive:** Emerald, Tanzanite, Opal, Amethyst, Citrine, Peridot, Topaz, Tourmaline, Amber, Zircon
- **Light-sensitive:** Amethyst (fades), Citrine (may fade), Kunzite (fades), Opal (crazing), Turquoise (darkens), Pearl (yellowing), Coral (bleaches), Topaz (some irradiated colors)
- **Chemical-sensitive:** Emerald (acids), Pearl (acids, cosmetics), Turquoise (oils, acids, ammonia), Lapis Lazuli (acids), Malachite (acids), Amber (solvents, alcohol, perfume), Opal (chemicals)

### GIA Durability Ratings

The Gemological Institute of America (GIA) classifies gem durability into four categories based on wearability:

1. **Excellent / Ideal for Daily Wear:** Diamond, Ruby, Sapphire, Spinel, Chrysoberyl (Alexandrite). Mohs ≥ 8, good toughness, stable.
2. **Good / Moderate Wear:** Amethyst, Citrine, Garnet, Aquamarine, Tourmaline, Topaz, Iolite, Peridot. Mohs 7–8. Suitable for most jewelry but remove during heavy activity.
3. **Fair / Occasional Wear:** Emerald, Opal, Tanzanite, Moonstone, Labradorite, Kunzite, Zircon. Mohs 6–7.5. Best for earrings, pendants, or protective settings.
4. **Poor / Minimal Wear:** Pearl, Coral, Amber, Malachite, Lapis Lazuli, Turquoise, Rhodochrosite, Azurite, Selenite. Mohs ≤ 6 or very fragile. Occasional wear only; avoid moisture, chemicals, impact.

## 2. Cleaning Methods

### Universal Safe Method: Warm Soapy Water + Soft Brush

The safest and most recommended cleaning method for virtually all hard, untreated gemstones:

- Use warm (not hot) water with mild liquid dish soap (e.g., Dawn)
- Soft nylon or natural-bristle brush
- Rinse thoroughly in clean water
- Pat dry with a lint-free, soft cloth

Best for: Diamond, Ruby, Sapphire, Spinel, Garnet, Quartz, Tourmaline, Chrysoberyl, Topaz (careful), Aquamarine, Peridot.

### Ultrasonic Cleaners

Ultrasonic cleaners use high-frequency sound waves and a cleaning solution to create cavitation bubbles that dislodge dirt. **Not all gems can tolerate the vibration.**

**Safe:** Diamond, Ruby (untreated), Sapphire (untreated), Garnet (all varieties), Spinel, Amethyst, Citrine, Aquamarine, Alexandrite.

**NEVER use with:**
- Emerald — fractures and oil will be damaged
- Tanzanite — brittle, prone to chipping along cleavage
- Turquoise — porous, vibration may crack
- Opal — can shatter or craze
- Pearl — surface damage, layer separation
- Topaz — perfect basal cleavage; vibration can cause fracture
- Peridot — brittle; may fracture
- Moonstone — cleavage and internal fractures
- Lapis Lazuli — porous, and pyrite inclusions may dislodge
- Malachite — soft, brittle
- Coral — fragile
- Amber — soft, solvent-sensitive
- **Any treated stone** — especially glass-filled, fracture-filled, diffusion-treated, dyed, oiled, or polymer-impregnated gems
- **Any stone with visible fractures, cavities, or heavy inclusions**

### Steam Cleaners

Steam cleaning uses high-pressure steam at temperatures exceeding 200°C. Even more aggressive than ultrasonic, with similar restrictions and additional heat sensitivity concerns.

**Safe:** Diamond, Ruby (untreated), Sapphire (untreated).

**NEVER use with:**
- All stones unsafe for ultrasonic (see above)
- Heat-sensitive stones: Emerald, Tanzanite, Opal, Amethyst, Citrine, Peridot, Zircon, Topaz, Amber
- Fracture-filled, glass-filled, dyed, oiled, coated, or polymer-impregnated stones
- Stones with thermal shock risk (especially Peridot, Zircon)

### Chemical Cleaners

**Never use on any gemstone:**
- Bleach (sodium hypochlorite) — damages gold settings and most gems
- Ammonia — safe only for diamond (and only occasionally); strip oil from emeralds, damage pearls, opals, turquoise
- Baking soda — too abrasive for polished surfaces
- Toothpaste — contains silica abrasives that will scratch most gems
- Acids (vinegar, lemon juice, etc.) — etch carbonates, dissolve pearl, damage lapis, malachite
- Acetone — dissolves oil/resin in emeralds, damages amber, organic gems
- Alcohol — may damage polymer-treated stones, amber, and organic gems

For diamonds only: a mild ammonia solution (1:5 with water) is occasionally acceptable.

## 4. Species-Specific Care

### Diamond (Mohs 10)

**Cleaning:** Warm soapy water, ultrasonic, or steam. Avoid chlorine bleach (damages settings, can pit diamonds). Handle by girdle when setting — avoid contact with skin oils during storage.

**Treated diamonds:** Laser-drilled, fracture-filled, and HPHT-annealed diamonds follow standard cleaning. Irradiated diamonds are stable. Coated diamonds (e.g., pink coating) need mild cleaning only.

**Storage:** Separate compartment — diamond scratches other gems and gold.

**Wearability:** Excellent for all jewelry types. Rings, bracelets, daily wear.

### Ruby & Sapphire (Corundum — Mohs 9)

**Cleaning:** Warm soapy water is safest. Ultrasonic and steam are safe only for untreated, low-inclusion stones.

**Treated stones:**
- **Glass-filled ruby:** Extremely delicate. No ultrasonic, no steam, no acids, no heat. Wipe with damp cloth only. Heat can melt the glass filler.
- **Diffusion-treated sapphire:** No ultrasonic, no recutting/polishing (removes thin color layer). Mild cleaning only.
- **Heat-treated:** Standard care — most corundum is heat-treated; safe for soap, ultrasonic, steam.

**Storage:** Separate from diamond and other gems (Mohs 9 can scratch lower stones). Avoid contact with hard objects that may chip facet junctions.

**Wearability:** Excellent for all settings. Daily-wear rings are fine.

### Emerald (Mohs 7.5–8)

**Cleaning:** Damp soft cloth only. Brief immersion in warm soapy water is acceptable but **do not soak**. No ultrasonic, no steam, no heat, no solvents.

**Oiled emeralds (standard treatment):** Cedar oil, Canada balsam, or artificial resins fill surface-reaching fractures. Solvents (acetone, alcohol) dissolve the oil. Heat causes expansion and oil loss.

**Polymer/resin-filled emeralds (ExCel, Opticon, Permasafe):** Even more sensitive. No solvents, no ultrasonic, no heat. The polymer can cloud or shrink.

**Storage:** Soft pouch, separate compartment. Avoid temperature extremes.

**Wearability:** Occasional wear. Pendants and earrings preferred over rings. Protective settings (bezel, halo) recommended for rings.

### Opal (Mohs 5.5–6.5)

**Cleaning:** Never submerge in water unless it's a hydrophane opal. Wipe gently with a damp, soft cloth. No ultrasonic, no steam, no chemicals.

**Stability:** Contains 5–10% water. Crazing (cracking) occurs from rapid dehydration or temperature change. Store in a humidified environment — a small cotton ball with distilled water in a sealed bag. Avoid dry climates (airtight storage is actually harmful; needs humidity).

**Doublets/triplets:** Never immerse in water — the glue layers will delaminate.

**Wearability:** Earrings and pendants only. Remove before hand washing. Not suitable for rings except protective settings and occasional wear.

### Pearl (Mohs 2.5–4.5)

**Cleaning:** Wipe with a soft damp cloth after each wear. No immersion, no ultrasonic, no steam, no chemicals. The nacre is delicate and porous.

**Avoid:** Acids (vinegar, citrus), cosmetics, perfume, hairspray, sweat. Pearls are organic — put them on after applying makeup and perfume.

**Storage:** Lay flat in a soft cloth or tissue. Do not store in airtight containers (pearls need moisture). Do not store with other gems (scratches easily). Restring periodically if on a strand — silk stretches and collects dirt.

**Wearability:** Occasional wear only. Remove before bathing, swimming, exercising.

### Turquoise (Mohs 5–6)

**Cleaning:** Wipe with a dry soft cloth. Never submerge, never use ultrasonic or steam. Turquoise is highly porous (15–25% porosity) and absorbs oils, lotions, and soap.

**Stabilization:** Most turquoise is stabilized with epoxy resin. Even so, avoid contact with chemicals, sunscreen, cosmetics. Unstabilized turquoise is extremely fragile.

**Color changes:** Absorbed oils darken turquoise. Some may green over time naturally.

**Storage:** Soft pouch, separate from harder gems. Avoid dry environments (may crack).

**Wearability:** Occasional wear. Pendants and earrings recommended. Remove rings before hand washing.

### Tanzanite (Mohs 6.5–7)

**Cleaning:** Warm soapy water, soft brush. No ultrasonic, no steam, no heat (even soldering can damage). Tanzanite has perfect cleavage in one direction and is brittle.

**Thermal shock:** A major risk — avoid sudden temperature changes. Jewelry repairs (soldering) must be done before the stone is set, or the stone must be removed.

**Storage:** Individual soft pouch. Never store loose together — tanzanite chips easily at facet junctions.

**Wearability:** Earrings and pendants. Not recommended for daily-wear rings (too soft, brittle). Protective settings extend life.

### Topaz (Mohs 8)

**Cleaning:** Warm soapy water, soft brush. No ultrasonic, no steam. Topaz has perfect basal cleavage — vibration or impact can split it along flat planes.

**Treated topaz:**
- **Irradiated blue topaz:** Stable after decay period (standard). Standard care.
- **Mystic topaz (coated):** No ultrasonic, no steam, no abrasives. The coating can be scratched or dissolved.
- **Heat-treated pink/orange topaz:** Standard care, stable.

**Storage:** Separate compartment. Impact to the pavilion or facet junctions can cause cleavage.

**Wearability:** Moderate. Rings are acceptable but remove during sports or heavy work. Earrings and pendants are safe.

### Peridot (Mohs 6.5–7)

**Cleaning:** Warm soapy water, soft brush. No ultrasonic, no steam. Peridot is brittle (iron-rich olivine) and sensitive to rapid temperature changes.

**Stability:** Not heat-sensitive per se, but thermal shock can fracture it. Avoid jeweler's torch near the stone.

**Weight loss:** Peridot can lose weight if left in hot water for long periods.

**Storage:** Soft pouch, separate compartment.

**Wearability:** Earrings and pendants. Ring use is possible but expect repolishing over time (it wears at facet edges).

### Aquamarine (Mohs 7.5–8)

**Cleaning:** Warm soapy water, soft brush. Ultrasonic and steam are generally safe for clean, inclusion-free stones.

**Heat treatment:** Most aquamarine is heat-treated to remove green tones. The treatment is stable.

**Stability:** Can fade in prolonged strong light. Generally quite durable for a beryl.

**Storage:** Separate compartment.

**Wearability:** Good for all settings, including rings. Avoid rough daily wear.

### Garnet (Mohs 6.5–7.5)

**Cleaning:** Warm soapy water, soft brush. Ultrasonic and steam are safe for most varieties (Almandine, Pyrope, Spessartite, Grossular, Tsavorite, Rhodolite, Demantoid).

**Stability:** Generally very stable across all varieties. Demantoid (green andradite) has a lower hardness (~6.5) and should be treated more gently.

**Storage:** Separate from harder gems (diamond, corundum).

**Wearability:** Excellent across settings. Rings are fine for most varieties. Demantoid should be in protective settings.

### Spinel (Mohs 8)

**Cleaning:** Warm soapy water, ultrasonic, or steam — all safe. Spinel is one of the toughest, most durable colored gems.

**Treated spinel:** Cobalt diffusion exists but is rare. Standard care, no ultrasonic if diffusion is suspected.

**Storage:** Separate compartment.

**Wearability:** Excellent. Suitable for daily-wear rings, bracelets, all settings.

### Tourmaline (Mohs 7–7.5)

**Cleaning:** Warm soapy water, soft brush. Exercise caution with ultrasonic and steam — only safe for clean, untreated, low-inclusion stones. Heavily included stones (common in tourmaline) may fracture.

**Heat treatment:** Common for pink/red to lighten or remove brown tones. Heat is stable. Irradiated green/pink tourmaline is also stable.

**Stability:** Piezoelectric and pyroelectric — generates charge under heat/pressure. Not an issue for care.

**Storage:** Individual soft pouch. Tourmaline can be brittle along lengthwise striae.

**Wearability:** Good for most settings. Rings are fine with care.

### Moonstone (Mohs 6–6.5)

**Cleaning:** Warm soapy water, soft brush. No ultrasonic, no steam, no heat.

**Internal structure:** Adularescence comes from feldspar lamellar structure. Shock or pressure can cause cleavage. Moonstone has poor toughness and perfect cleavage in two directions.

**Storage:** Cushioned, separate compartment. Never stack moonstone rings.

**Wearability:** Earrings and pendants best. Rings are risky unless in a protective bezel setting.

### Alexandrite (Mohs 8.5)

**Cleaning:** Warm soapy water, ultrasonic, or steam — all safe. Very durable, stable chrysoberyl.

**Synthetic:** Flux or Czochralski-grown synthetic alexandrite has the same chemical properties and care requirements.

**Storage:** Separate from diamond (diamond scratches everything).

**Wearability:** Excellent for all settings. Daily-wear rings are suitable.

### Citrine (Mohs 7)

**Cleaning:** Warm soapy water, ultrasonic, or steam — all safe.

**Color stability:** May fade in prolonged strong sunlight. Most citrine is heat-treated amethyst; treatment is stable.

**Storage:** Separate compartment.

**Wearability:** Good for all settings. Rings are fine.

### Amethyst (Mohs 7)

**Cleaning:** Warm soapy water, ultrasonic, or steam — all safe.

**Color stability:** Fades in prolonged direct sunlight or UV exposure. Not suitable for always-on rings in sun-rich environments.

**Heat treatment:** Heat turns amethyst yellow→green→colorless. Avoid jeweler's torch directly on the stone.

**Storage:** Separate compartment.

**Wearability:** Good for all settings. Suitable for rings with awareness of light fading.

### Jadeite & Nephrite (Jade — Mohs 6–7)

**Cleaning:** Warm soapy water, soft brush. Ultrasonic and steam are risky for treated jade (B-jade with polymer impregnation).

**Type A (natural, untreated):** Soak in water occasionally — jade appreciates moisture. Standard care.

**Type B (polymer-impregnated, bleached):** No ultrasonic, no steam, no heat, no solvents. The polymer can yellow, cloud, or degrade.

**Type C (dyed):** No soaking, no chemicals, no ultrasonic. Dye bleeds with moisture.

**Storage:** Separate compartment. Very tough (especially nephrite — the toughest natural gem) but can still be scratched by harder gems.

**Wearability:** Excellent for all settings. Nephrite bangles are traditional and can withstand heavy wear.

### Lapis Lazuli (Mohs 5.5)

**Cleaning:** Warm soapy water, soft brush (brief). No ultrasonic, no steam, no chemicals (acids dissolve calcite component).

**Stability:** Porous. Pyrite inclusions can tarnish and dislodge if exposed to moisture.

**Storage:** Dry, separate compartment. Avoid direct sun (color may fade).

**Wearability:** Earrings and pendants. Rings are possible with protective settings, but the stone will abrade over time.

### Malachite (Mohs 3.5–4)

**Cleaning:** Damp soft cloth only — wipe gently. No water immersion, no ultrasonic, no steam, no chemicals.

**Sensitivity:** Acid-sensitive — reacts with even mild acids (vinegar skin pH). Porous and soft. Pattern wear may occur on rings.

**Toxicity:** **WARNING:** Malachite contains copper compounds. Do not soak in water — dissolved copper can irritate skin. Do not use in gem elixirs.

**Storage:** Soft cloth wrap. Separate from harder gems.

**Wearability:** Earrings, pendants, brooches. Not suitable for rings (too soft).

### Coral (Mohs 3–4)

**Cleaning:** Wipe with dry or barely damp soft cloth. No water, no ultrasonic, no steam, no chemicals.

**Sensitivity:** Porous organic material (calcium carbonate). Acids dissolve it. Bleaches in sun. Absorbs oils, perfume, cosmetics.

**Storage:** Lay flat in soft tissue. Do not store in plastic bags (traps moisture).

**Wearability:** Occasional wear only. Pendants, earrings. Remove before bathing, swimming, exercising.

### Amber (Mohs 2–2.5)

**Cleaning:** Wipe with dry soft cloth. Slightly damp cloth if needed, then dry immediately. No water immersion, no ultrasonic, no steam, no chemicals.

**Sensitivity:** Fossilized tree resin. Soluble in alcohol, acetone, perfume, solvents. Scratches very easily. Becomes brittle with age and dehydration. Avoid heat (melts at ~150–200°C).

**Storage:** Soft pouch, temperature-controlled environment (not hot attics, not freezing). Do not store with hard gems.

**Wearability:** Occasional wear only. Pendants and earrings. Not suitable for rings (too soft).

### Zircon (Mohs 7–7.5)

**Cleaning:** Warm soapy water, soft brush only. No ultrasonic, no steam. Zircon is brittle, and facet junctions chip easily.

**Heat sensitivity:** Heat-treated zircons (most common blue) can revert to brownish if overheated. Avoid jeweler's torch.

**Stability:** Metamict zircons (from radioactive decay) may be amorphous and even more fragile.

**Storage:** Individual padded compartment. Never allow zircon to touch other stones.

**Wearability:** Earrings and pendants best. Rings are possible with protective settings and occasional wear.

### Iolite (Mohs 7–7.5)

**Cleaning:** Warm soapy water, soft brush. No ultrasonic, no steam. Iolite is pleochroic (three colors in one) and brittle along cleavage planes.

**Storage:** Soft pouch, separate compartment.

**Wearability:** Moderate. Earrings and pendants are best. Rings with protective settings are acceptable.

### Spodumene (Kunzite, Hiddenite — Mohs 6.5–7)

**Cleaning:** Warm soapy water, soft brush. No ultrasonic, no steam.

**Stability:** Perfect cleavage. Heat and strong light fade color (especially kunzite). Avoid prolonged sun exposure.

**Storage:** Cushioned, separate compartment.

**Wearability:** Earrings and pendants only. Too fragile for daily-wear rings.

### Chrysoprase (Mohs 6.5–7)

**Cleaning:** Warm soapy water, soft brush. No ultrasonic, no steam, no heat.

**Sensitivity:** Nickel-containing chalcedony. Can fade in strong light. Porous; avoid chemicals.

**Storage:** Soft pouch. Avoid very dry conditions (may dehydrate and lighten).

**Wearability:** Good for pendants and earrings. Rings acceptable with care.

### Bloodstone, Carnelian, Agate, Onyx, Jasper (Chalcedony — Mohs 6.5–7)

**Cleaning:** Warm soapy water, ultrasonic, or steam — generally safe for natural, undyed chalcedony.

**Dyed stones:** Many agates, carnelians, and jaspers are dyed. Dyed stones: no soaking, no ultrasonic, no steam.

**Stability:** Very durable for quartz microcrystals. Excellent toughness.

**Storage:** Separate compartment.

**Wearability:** Excellent for all settings.

### Rhodochrosite (Mohs 3.5–4.5)

**Cleaning:** Wipe with damp soft cloth. No water immersion, no ultrasonic, no steam, no chemicals. Soft and acid-sensitive.

**Stability:** Cleavage in three directions — extremely fragile for jewelry. Often used in cabochons only.

**Storage:** Soft padded compartment. Never with other gems.

**Wearability:** Earrings and pendants, occasional wear only. Not suitable for rings.

### Apatite (Mohs 5)

**Cleaning:** Warm soapy water, soft brush. No ultrasonic, no steam. Apatite is soft and brittle.

**Storage:** Soft pouch, separate compartment.

**Wearability:** Earrings and pendants only. Rarely used in rings.

### Larimar (Mohs 4.5–5)

**Cleaning:** Warm soapy water, soft brush. No ultrasonic, no steam, no chemicals.

**Storage:** Soft pouch. Avoid prolonged sun exposure (color may fade).

**Wearability:** Earrings, pendants. Not suitable for rings.

### Variscite (Mohs 4–5)

**Cleaning:** Damp soft cloth. No water immersion, no ultrasonic, no steam. Porous, similar to turquoise.

**Storage:** Soft pouch, separate from harder gems.

**Wearability:** Occasional wear, pendants and earrings.

## 5. Storage Recommendations

### General Principles

1. **Hardness separation:** Store gems of similar hardness together. Diamond (10), Ruby/Sapphire (9), and Spinel/Alexandrite (8–8.5) can scratch all other gems. Use separate compartments or individual pouches.
2. **Loose stones:** Never store loose stones together in a single baggie — they abrade and chip each other. Use individual padded compartments or wrapped parcels.
3. **Necklaces:** Lay flat or hang with minimal tension on a padded rod. Avoid kinking chains.
4. **Rings:** Use ring slots in a padded box. Do not stack rings on top of each other.
5. **Tissue/acidity:** Use acid-free tissue paper. Avoid cotton wool (can snag prongs).

### Humidity Control

| Gem | Humidity Preference | Method |
|-----|-------------------|--------|
| Opal | 60–80% RH (high) | Cotton ball with distilled water in sealed bag; commercial humidity pack |
| Turquoise | 40–60% RH (moderate) | Avoid extreme dryness; small humidipak |
| Pearl | 40–70% RH (moderate) | Lay flat, never airtight; silica gel only if carefully monitored |
| Jade | 40–60% RH | Occasional water soak for Type A jade |
| Amber | 40–50% RH | Avoid extremes; temperature-stable environment |
| Most others | 30–50% RH | Standard room conditions are fine |

### What to Avoid

- **Direct sunlight** — fades amethyst, citrine, kunzite, rose quartz, opal, topaz
- **Heat sources** — radiators, direct heat gun, prolonged car interior
- **Plastic bags** (except short-term) — trap moisture, promote mold on porous gems
- **Velvet-lined trays** long-term — may shed fibers or contain sulfur compounds that tarnish metals
- **Rubber bands** near jewelry — sulfur in rubber tarnishes silver and gold

## 6. Wearability by Setting Type

### Rings (Highest Impact)

Gems in rings receive the most impact, friction, and chemical exposure of any jewelry type.

**Recommended (daily wear, Mohs ≥ 8):**
- Diamond, Ruby, Sapphire, Spinel, Alexandrite, Chrysoberyl

**Acceptable (with care, Mohs 7–8):**
- Aquamarine, Garnet, Amethyst, Citrine, Tourmaline, Topaz, Iolite, Zircon
- Use protective settings (bezel, halo, recessed)
- Remove for sports, gardening, gym, dishwashing

**Not recommended (Mohs < 7 or poor toughness):**
- Opal, Pearl, Turquoise, Tanzanite, Peridot, Moonstone, Malachite, Coral, Amber, Lapis Lazuli, Jadeite (fine for bangles/tumbled jade but not faceted rings), Rhodochrosite

### Earrings (Lowest Impact)

Minimal impact exposure — most gems are safe in earrings.

**Safe:** All gems except the softest (amber, coral, pearl — use protective settings). Even tanzanite, opal, moonstone, and emerald are fine in earrings.

**Precautions:**
- Ensure secure settings — leverbacks > pushbacks for valuable stones
- Opals and pearls in earrings should be wiped after wear (skin contact, perfume)

### Pendants / Necklaces (Low Impact)

Even easier than earrings — most gems are safe here.

**Safe:** All gems. Even fragile stones like rhodochrosite, amber, and coral can be worn daily as pendants.

**Precautions:**
- Pearls on silk — restring every 1–2 years
- Opals — avoid wearing over bare skin in winter (dry heat from body causes crazing)
- Ensure bails/bezels are secure for heavy cabochons

### Bracelets (High Impact)

Similar to rings — frequent impact against surfaces.

**Recommended (Mohs ≥ 7):**
- Diamond, Ruby, Sapphire, Spinel, Garnet, Quartz, Jade bangles

**Acceptable:**
- Emerald, Tourmaline, Aquamarine — only in protective settings

**Not recommended:**
- Tanzanite, Opal, Pearl, Turquoise, Peridot, Moonstone, Malachite, Coral, Amber, Lapis Lazuli

**Bangle-specific:** Jadeite and nephrite bangles are traditional and durable due to toughness. Ensure correct size — too tight invites impact fractures.

## 7. Treatment-Specific Care

### Glass-Filled Ruby

Extremely common in lower-grade ruby. Glass filling hides surface-reaching fractures.

- **No ultrasonic** — vibration dislodges filler
- **No steam** — high pressure + heat damages filler
- **No acids** — even mild acids degrade glass filler
- **No heat** — soldering, hot water may melt or cloud filler (glass filler melts ~600°C, but thermal stress cracks it)
- **No polishing/recutting** — exposes unfilled areas
- **Cleaning:** Damp soft cloth only, gentle wipe
- **Detection:** Flash effect (blue/orange flashes in reflected light), gas bubbles in filler

### Oiled / Resin-Filled Emerald

Standard practice for most emeralds to reduce visible fractures.

- **No ultrasonic** — oil is displaced
- **No steam** — heat dramatically expands oil, pushing it out
- **No solvents** (acetone, alcohol) — dissolves organic oils
- **Avoid hot water** — warms oil, reduces surface tension, leaks out
- **Cleaning:** Damp soft cloth; brief lukewarm soapy dip (1–2 seconds max)
- **Re-oiling:** Expected periodically (every 1–5 years depending on wear)
- **Detection:** UV fluorescence (oils fluoresce blue-white)

### Dyed Stones

Common in turquoise, jade (Type C), agate, chalcedony, coral, howlite, lapis lazuli.

- **No soaking** — dye bleeds into water
- **No ultrasonic** — vibration dislodges dye
- **No chemicals** — oils, acids, ammonia extract dye
- **No steam**
- **No direct sunlight** — some dyes photodegrade
- **Cleaning:** Dry or barely damp cloth only
- **Detection:** Dye concentration in cracks, UV fluorescence, acetone swab test

### Diffusion-Treated Sapphire

Beryllium or titanium diffusion creates thin color layers (typically <1 mm).

- **No recutting/polishing** — removes color layer entirely
- **No ultrasonic** — may compromise shallow color zone in fractures
- **No strong heat** — may alter diffusion layer
- **Cleaning:** Gentle warm soapy water
- **Detection:** Color concentration at facet edges, flat color distribution

### Fracture-Filled Diamond

Lead-glass or silicate filler in laser-drilled or natural fractures.

- **No ultrasonic** — filler is dislodged
- **No steam** — heat damages filler
- **No heat** — filler expands differently, causing fracture extension
- **No acids** — etch glass filler
- **Cleaning:** Gentle soapy water, soft brush
- **Detection:** Flash effect in fractures

### Irradiated Topaz

Most blue topaz is irradiated (neutron or electron beam).

- **Stable** after decay period — standard topaz care applies
- **Heat:** Avoid torches — color may shift, but irradiated blue is more stable than previously thought
- **Storage:** Standard
- **Detection:** No standard test for irradiation; requires combination of spectroscopic analysis

### HPHT Diamond

High-Pressure High-Temperature annealing improves color in brown diamonds.

- **Same care as natural diamond**
- **Detection:** Requires spectroscopy (FTIR, PL) and DiamondView for growth patterns

### CVD Diamond

Chemical Vapor Deposition grown synthetic diamond.

- **Same care as natural diamond**
- **Detection:** Photoluminescence (NV centers, SiV), DiamondView (striated growth patterns)

### Polymer-Impregnated Jadeite (B-Jade)

Bleached and polymer-injected to improve clarity.

- **No ultrasonic** — polymer degrades under vibration
- **No steam** — high heat melts polymer
- **No heat** (>100°C) — polymer shrinks, clouds, yellows
- **No solvents** (alcohol, acetone) — dissolve polymer
- **Avoid prolonged UV** — yellowing over years
- **Cleaning:** Damp soft cloth only

### Surface-Coated Stones

Mystic topaz (titanium coating), coated sapphire, coated tanzanite.

- **No recutting/polishing** — removes coating
- **No ultrasonic** — vibration flakes coating
- **No abrasives** — scratches coating
- **No chemicals** — may react with coating
- **Cleaning:** Warm soapy water, very soft cloth, gentle pat dry
- **Detection:** Colored surface reflections, wear at facet edges

### Composite Stones (Doublets, Triplets, Intarsia)

Opal doublets/triplets, garnet-topped doublets, assembled stones.

- **Never immerse in water** — delamination of glue layers
- **No solvents, no heat, no ultrasonic, no steam**
- **Cleaning:** Damp or dry soft cloth
- **Detection:** Obvious glue line side-view, flat joint plane
