# 2. Chelsea Colour Filter Reaction Table

> **Principle:** A dichromatic filter transmitting ~570 nm (yellow-green) and ~620–700 nm (red). Functions as a rapid chromium (Cr³⁺) vs. iron (Fe²⁺/Fe³⁺) discriminator. **Must be used under incandescent (tungsten) light only — LED sources produce unreliable results.**

## 2.1 Green Stones — Chelsea Filter

| Species | CCF Reaction | Chromophore | Diagnostic Strength | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Emerald (Colombian/Brazilian) | **Strong red** | Cr³⁺ | High | High Cr; classic positive |
| Emerald (Zambian) | Weak red to inert | Cr³⁺ + Fe | Moderate | Elevated Fe suppresses red |
| Synthetic emerald (Chatham, Gilson, Biron, Tairus) | **Strong red** | Cr³⁺ | High | High-purity Cr; often stronger than naturals |
| Demantoid garnet | Red to reddish | Cr³⁺ | High | Cr³⁺ colouring |
| Chrome tourmaline | **Red** | Cr³⁺ | High | Cr-bearing; resembles emerald |
| Tsavorite garnet | Inert to weak greenish | V³⁺ | Moderate | V³⁺ absorption differs from Cr³⁺ |
| Green tourmaline (Fe/Mn) | Greenish / inert | Fe / Mn | High | No significant Cr |
| Aquamarine | Inert / weak greenish | Fe²⁺ / Fe³⁺ | High | Fe-coloured |
| Peridot | Inert | Fe²⁺ | High | Fe-coloured |
| Jadeite (natural Cr-coloured) | Weak greenish to inert | Cr³⁺ (low) | Low–Moderate | Low Cr content |
| Jadeite (dyed green, Type C) | **Red** | Organic dye | High | Dye diagnostic |
| Aventurine quartz | Reddish | Fuchsite (Cr-mica) | Moderate | Inclusion-based |
| Chrysoprase | Green | Ni | Moderate | Nickel-coloured |
| Enstatite | Green | Fe | Moderate | Fe-coloured |
| Hiddenite (Spodumene) | Reddish | Cr³⁺ | Moderate | Cr-bearing spodumene |
| Alexandrite (in daylight) | Red | Cr³⁺ | High | Strong Cr absorption |
| Uvarovite garnet | Red | Cr³⁺ | High | Cr-coloured |

## 2.4 Critical Chelsea Filter Rules

1. CCF **detects chromium, not origin** — natural and synthetic Cr-rich stones react identically
2. **Vanadium (V³⁺)** -coloured stones (many Brazilian emeralds, tsavorite) give weak/inert reactions
3. **Incandescent (tungsten) light only** — LED and daylight produce unreliable/false results
4. CCF is a **screening tool only** — always confirm with RI, SG, and spectroscope

# 4. Treatment Detection Manual

## 4.1 Heat Treatment

| Attribute | Details |
|---|---|
| **Commonly applied to** | Ruby, sapphire (all colours), tanzanite, aquamarine, citrine (amethyst → citrine), amethyst, kunzite, topaz, zircon, tourmaline, morganite |
| **Diagnostic visual cues** | • **Colour**: more uniform, saturation increased; zoning softened<br>• **Inclusions**: discoid fractures (halos) around crystal inclusions in corundum<br>• **Silk**: originally straight rutile needles become globular, discontinuous, or dissolve entirely in corundum<br>• **Surface**: frosted appearance around inclusions |
| **UV effect (corundum)** | • Many heat-treated corundum shows **chalky bluish-white SWUV** fluorescence (especially blue/yellow sapphire)<br>• Untreated Ceylon blue sapphire shows **orange/red LWUV**; heat-treated loses this<br>• "Apricot" orange fluorescence (Sri Lanka, Madagascar) — unaffected by heat |
| **Magnetic susceptibility** | Heat reduces magnetic susceptibility in corundum (Fe²⁺/Fe³⁺ conversion) |

## 4.2 Fracture Filling / Oiling

| Attribute | Details |
|---|---|
| **Commonly applied to** | Emerald (oil, resin), ruby (lead glass filling), diamond (fracture filling), turquoise (resin stabilization), opal |
| **Diagnostic visual cues** | • **Flash effects**: iridescent rainbow or blue-green flashes when tilted (10x–60x)<br>• Flow structures within fissures<br>• Trapped air bubbles in filler<br>• **Surface-reaching fissures** with distinctly coloured filler |
| **UV effect** | • Oil/resin in emerald: **yellow to orange-yellow fluorescence** under LWUV<br>• Lead glass in ruby: may show unusual fluorescence |
| **Emerald fillers** | • Cedarwood oil: weak greenish-yellow<br>• Canada balsam: weak yellow<br>• Opticon (resin): moderate yellow LWUV<br>• Polymer resins: moderate to strong yellow/orange LWUV |

## 4.3 Irradiation

| Attribute | Details |
|---|---|
| **Commonly applied to** | Topaz (colourless → blue), diamond (green, pink, blue, black), quartz (→ smoky), pearl (grey/black), kunzite (→ green), beryl (→ maxixe blue), spodumene |
| **Diagnostic visual cues** | • **Blue topaz**: strong even blue (natural blue topaz is extremely rare and pale)<br>• **Colour-zoned diamonds**: green colour often restricted to near-surface layer<br>• **Radioactive halo**: colour darker near surface in some stones |
| **Detection** | • Blue topaz: considered diagnostic — natural blue topaz is extremely rare<br>• Diamond: 504 nm, 537 nm, 595 nm spectroscope lines after irradiation<br>• Colour may be permanent (blue topaz) or may fade (pink kunzite) |

## 4.4 Surface Diffusion / Coating

| Attribute | Details |
|---|---|
| **Commonly applied to** | Corundum (Ti/Be diffusion for colour), Mystic Topaz (metallic coating), beryl (coating for emerald colour), quartz, opal (smoke treatment) |
| **Diagnostic visual cues** | • **Colour concentration** in facet junctions and surface (visible in immersion)<br>• **Tilt effect**: colour appears 2D — lies on surface<br>• **Scratches and abrasions** show different colour underneath<br>• Coating may peel at facet edges |
| **Detection** | • **Immersion in RI fluid**: colour concentrated at surface only<br>• **Magnification**: coating wear on facet junctions<br>• Be-diffused corundum: colour appears "hanging" in facet junctions |

## 4.5 Dyeing

| Attribute | Details |
|---|---|
| **Commonly applied to** | Chalcedony (agate, chrysoprase), jadeite (Type C), turquoise, lapis lazuli, pearl, quartzite, howlite |
| **Diagnostic visual cues** | • Colour concentrated in **fractures and porous areas**<br>• Uneven colour distribution<br>• Unnatural colour intensity for the species |
| **CCF effect** | Dyed green jadeite: **red** (natural jadeite: inert to weak greenish) |
| **UV effect** | Organic dyes often fluoresce more strongly than natural colour |
| **Detection** | Acetone swab may pick up dye (slightly destructive); magnification shows colour pooling |

## 4.6 HPHT (High Pressure High Temperature)

| Attribute | Details |
|---|---|
| **Commonly applied to** | Diamond (colour improvement — brown → colourless or fancy colours) |
| **Diagnostic visual cues** | • Reduction of brown colour; may leave graphite inclusions now transparent<br>• **Strain patterns** visible under cross-polarized light<br>• Colour zoning may be irregular |
| **UV effect** | • **SWUV: stronger than LWUV** (opposite of natural)<br>• **Phosphorescence**: often present (blue/green)<br>• Type IIa diamonds: phosphorescence diagnostic |
| **Detection** | Usually requires advanced lab testing (FTIR, UV-Vis, PL spectroscopy) |

## 4.7 Polymer / Resin Impregnation

| Attribute | Details |
|---|---|
| **Commonly applied to** | Turquoise (stabilization), jadeite (Type B), opal, emerald (some) |
| **Diagnostic visual cues** | • **Jadeite Type B**: "chicken pox" pitted surface after acid bleaching; glassy unnatural appearance<br>• **Turquoise**: harder than natural; may feel plastic-like<br>• Density lower than natural (polymer is lighter) |
| **Detection** | • **Hot point test**: polymer melts/smells (slightly destructive)<br>• UV fluorescence: patchy glow<br>• IR spectroscopy diagnostic |

# 7. Optical Phenomena Encyclopedia

## 7.1 Refraction Type

| Type | Definition | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Isotropic (SR)** | Single Refraction — light travels at same speed in all directions | Diamond, spinel, garnet, fluorite, CZ, glass, opal, amber |
| **Anisotropic (DR)** | Double Refraction — light splits into two rays | Most gemstones |
| **Aggregate** | Polycrystalline — composed of many small crystals | Jadeite, nephrite, turquoise, chalcedony, lapis lazuli |

## 7.2 Birefringence Strength

| Strength | ΔRI | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Weak | 0.003–0.010 | Beryl, corundum, apatite, topaz, chrysoberyl |
| Moderate | 0.010–0.030 | Tourmaline, peridot, diopside, spodumene, tanzanite |
| Strong | 0.030–0.060 | Peridot (0.036), zircon (0.059), sinhalite (0.038), diaspore (0.048) |
| Very strong | 0.060–0.287 | Calcite (0.172), sphene (0.135), cassiterite (0.098), rutile (0.287) |

## 7.3 Dispersion (Fire)

| Strength | Value (B–G interval) | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Very low | < 0.010 | Quartz (0.013), beryl (0.014), corundum (0.018) |
| Low | 0.010–0.020 | Topaz (0.014), tourmaline (0.017), feldspar (0.012) |
| Moderate | 0.020–0.030 | Spinel (0.020), garnet (0.022–0.028), chrysoberyl (0.015) |
| High | 0.030–0.060 | Zircon (0.038), demantoid (0.057), sphene (0.051), CZ (0.060) |
| Very high | > 0.060 | Moissanite (0.104), synthetic rutile (0.280), strontium titanate (0.190) |

## 7.4 Pleochroism

| Strength | Definition | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **None** | No colour change with direction | Isotropic (SR) gems — diamond, garnet, spinel |
| **Weak** | Subtle hue shift | Emerald (blue-green/yellow-green), aquamarine, most sapphire |
| **Moderate** | Clear colour difference | Ruby (purplish-red/orangey-red), tourmaline, topaz, alexandrite |
| **Strong** | Dramatic colour change | **Tanzanite** (blue/violet/burgundy) — **trichroic**, **Iolite** (dark blue/light blue/yellow-brown) — **trichroic**, **Andalusite** (olive green/red-brown/yellow-green) — **trichroic** |

## 7.5 Special Optical Phenomena

| Phenomenon | Definition | Cause | Seen In |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Adularescence** | Floating bluish-white glow that moves across surface | Light scattering from alternating feldspar lamellae | Moonstone |
| **Labradorescence** | Metallic colour flash (schiller) at specific angles | Light interference from exsolution lamellae | Labradorite, spectrolite |
| **Chatoyancy (Cat's Eye)** | Sharp reflective band perpendicular to cabochon's length | Parallel aligned fibrous/needle inclusions | Chrysoberyl (finest), tourmaline, apatite, diopside, quartz, cat's-eye scapolite |
| **Asterism** | 4 or 6-ray star across cabochon dome | Reflected light from oriented rutile or haematite needles | **6-ray**: Ruby, sapphire, rose quartz; **4-ray**: Star garnet, star diopside |
| **Play-of-Colour** | Shifting spectral colours within the stone | Diffraction of light from regularly packed silica spheres (~150–300 nm) | Precious opal (black opal provides best contrast) |
| **Colour Change** | Different body colour under daylight vs incandescent | Selective absorption peaks balanced between two wavelength regions | Alexandrite (green/red — Cr³⁺); sapphire (blue/purple — V); garnet (brownish/red — V, Cr); diaspore (greenish/pinkish) |
| **Iridescence** | Rainbow colour on internal surfaces or surface | Thin-film interference on fractures or exsolution | Rainbow moonstone, fire agate, obsidian with hematite |
| **Aventurescence** | Sparkly metallic glitter effect | Reflective platelet inclusions (mica, haematite, goethite) | Aventurine quartz (fuchsite mica), sunstone (haematite/goethite) |
| **Phosphorescence** | Glow continues after UV excitation removed | Electron traps in crystal defects | Diamond (HPHT synthetic — common; natural — rare), hackmanite, opal (some), sphalerite |
| **Tenebrescence** | Reversible colour change under UV/sunlight (fades in dark) | Colour centres altered by UV exposure | Hackmanite (sodalite var.), tugtupite, scapolite |
| **Fluorescence** | Glow during UV excitation | Trace elements (Cr³⁺, Mn²⁺, U⁶⁺) or structural defects | Ruby (red), diamond (blue), fluorite (blue), scheelite (bright blue-white SW) |
| **Opalescence** | Milky/pearly sheen (often confused with play-of-colour) | Light scattering from sub-microscopic structures | Opal, moonstone |
| **Change of Colour** (not colour-change) | Different colour in different crystal directions in DR gems | Pleochroism; confused with colour-change | Iolite, tanzanite, andalusite |

# 8. Polariscope Interpretation

| Observation at 360° rotation | Interpretation | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Completely dark (extinct)** at all angles | Isotropic — SR | Diamond, spinel, garnet, CZ, glass, opal, fluorite, amber |
| **Light, dark, light, dark** — 4 extinction positions | Anisotropic — DR | Most gemstones: corundum, quartz, beryl, tourmaline, topaz, peridot |
| **Mottled or snake-like pattern** — no complete extinction | Aggregate — polycrystalline | Jadeite, nephrite, turquoise, lapis lazuli, chalcedony, agate |
| **Iris or bullseye pattern** under conoscopic interference | Uniaxial | Quartz, corundum, tourmaline, zircon, beryl |
| **Two moving isogyre arms** under conoscopic interference | Biaxial | Topaz, peridot, tanzanite, chrysoberyl, kyanite, spodumene |
| **No complete extinction, anomalous colours** | Anomalous DR (ADR) — stone is strained | Common in some spinel, synthetic corundum, glass, fluorite |
| **ADR (tabby extinction)** — wavy/vague extinction | Strain from heat, rapid growth, or synthetic | Many synthetic spinels, flame fusion corundum |

# 10. Field Testing Procedures

## 10.1 Using the Refractometer

1. Ensure the instrument is calibrated (read known standard: quartz = 1.544–1.553)
2. Apply **one small drop** of contact liquid (RI fluid) to the hemicylinder centre
3. Place the stone **table-down** on the hemicylinder (largest facet in contact)
4. Illuminate with a small flashlight at the correct angle
5. Read the RI value from the scale
6. For DR stones: rotate the polarizer filter to read **lowest and highest** RI
7. Calculate birefringence: difference between lowest and highest RI
8. **Clean stone and hemicylinder immediately after** — RI fluids can damage some materials
9. **RI fluids**: 1.79 (standard), 1.81 (high RI for corundum/spinel), 1.70 (sapphire/spinel — safe alternative)
10. **Contact liquid cleaner**: isopropyl alcohol on lens tissue — never acetone

## 10.2 Specific Gravity — Hydrostatic Weighing

The hydrostatic method measures SG by weighing a stone in air and suspended in water.
1. Weigh the stone in air (**A**) to two decimal places
2. Suspend the stone in **distilled water at room temperature** using a fine wire/thread
3. Record the weight in water (**W**)
4. **Formula:** SG = A ÷ (A – W)
5. For best accuracy: ensure stone is clean, no air bubbles, and use distilled water
6. **Correction for temperature:** water density at 20°C = 0.998 g/cm³ (usually negligible for field work)

## 10.3 Specific Gravity — Heavy Liquids (Immersion Test)

| Liquid | SG | Stones float (below SG) | Stones sink (above SG) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bromoform** | 2.89 | Quartz (2.65), feldspar (2.56–2.70), beryl (2.72), opal (2.00–2.25) | Corundum (4.00), spinel (3.60), tourmaline (3.06), garnet (3.6–4.3) |
| **Methylene iodide** | 3.32 | Tourmaline (3.06), beryl (2.72), quartz (2.65), apatite (3.20) | Corundum (4.00), spinel (3.60), garnet (3.6–4.3), zircon (4.69) |
| **Clerici solution** | ~4.15 (adjustable) | Most gems: corundum (4.00), spinel (3.60), chrysoberyl (3.73) | Almandine (4.05), spessartite (4.15), zircon (4.69), CZ (5.80) |

> **Caution:** Methylene iodide and bromoform are toxic. Clerici solution is highly toxic and corrosive. Work in a ventilated area with gloves.

## 10.4 Using the Dichroscope

1. Hold the dichroscope aperture against the stone under a strong light
2. Rotate the dichroscope 90° to observe colour change
3. **Two colours** = dichroic (uniaxial: tetragonal, hexagonal, trigonal)
4. **Three colours** = trichroic (biaxial: orthorhombic, monoclinic, triclinic)
5. **No colour difference** = isotropic (cubic) or weak DR

| Uniaxial (2 colours) | Biaxial (3 colours, but need rotation to see all) |
|---|---|
| Ruby: purplish-red / orangey-red | Tanzanite: blue / violet / burgundy |
| Sapphire: light blue / dark blue | Iolite: dark blue / light blue / yellow-brown |
| Emerald: bluish-green / yellowish-green | Andalusite: olive green / red-brown / yellow-green |
| Tourmaline: colour very strong / very weak | Alexandrite: green / red / orange-red |
| Quartz: very weak (hard to see) | Topaz: colourless / light yellow / yellow |

## 10.5 Using the Chelsea Filter (CCF)

1. Use **incandescent (tungsten) light only** — never LED or fluorescent
2. Hold the filter to your eye
3. Hold stone close to (but not touching) the light source
4. Observe and record: strong red / weak red / pink / inert / weak greenish / green
5. **Always cross-reference** with RI and spectroscope

## 10.6 Using the UV Lamp

1. Perform in **complete darkness**
2. Allow eyes to adjust to dark for at least 30 seconds
3. Hold stone directly at the lamp window (or as near as possible)
4. Test **both LW (365nm) and SW (254nm)** — they often differ
5. Protect eyes and skin from SW exposure (especially with high-intensity lamps)
6. Record: colour and strength (none / weak / moderate / strong)
7. Check for **phosphorescence** by removing stone from SWUV immediately and observing for seconds after
8. **SW lamp filter degrades** over time (~400 hours of life) — replace annually

## 10.7 Magnification (10x Loupe / Microscope)

| Technique | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Darkfield**: light from side against dark background | Best for seeing inclusions, growth lines, gas bubbles |
| **Overhead (direct) light** | Surface features, facet edges, polish quality |
| **Immersion**: stone in RI fluid or water | Colour zoning clarity, internal strain, doublets |
| **Oblique lighting**: light from one angle | Relief of inclusions, host crystal identification |
| **Fiber optic** | Deep illumination of internal features |
